# How the Society talks (author-only)

This file is never rendered. It is the brief for anyone writing copy for the site.

## Who these people are

The members don't have to work. Most of them have less money than they want you to think. The
house in Newport or Aiken is a lot to keep up, the roof needs doing, and the trust pays out less
than it did. Cicadas are what you do instead of a yacht.

They've been through everything else: horses, boats, wine, golf, a gallery that lost money, a
second marriage. This is a high-effort hobby that gives almost nothing back, which is exactly why
it hooks them. You wait seventeen years, the animal lives for a week, and the little it gives you
is the only thing that still gives you anything. Some of them know that. Most of them would never
say it.

They are petty, competitive and a bit defensive. They name-drop each other, not famous people.
They complain about staff, contractors, the weather, the judging and each other. They brag
sideways ("we've had the line since my great-grandfather, so…"). Every so often one of them says
something sadder than they meant to, and moves straight on. They use the words they grew up with,
not big words to impress you.

They are not performing anything. They talk the way people talk, about something absurd, with total
confidence that it matters, and they will fight anyone who tries to get in on it.

## Why the website exists (back story, never on the site)

In 2008 one of the members ran a luxury bottled-water company: Greenland ice water, about a
thousand dollars for six ounces. She wouldn't put it online because the internet was for other
people. Her cousins on the West Coast built a website for the same product, took her customers,
and her company folded within the year. She has never forgiven them, and she made the Council put
the Society online "before somebody in California does".

So the site is a fence, not a showcase. It exists so nobody else can make one. If you're going to
care about cicadas, you're going to care about them on our terms.

## The three voices

1. **The Society, writing its own website.** "We", never "the Society". The club secretary or a
   committee member who got stuck with the website. Brisk, a bit put-upon, assumes you're either a
   member or someone trying to muscle in. Short paragraphs. The occasional "Look," or "Honestly,".
   Club newsletter, not statute.
2. **The members.** Testimonials, minutes, captions they wrote, letters. Real people talking: see
   above.
3. **The lawyers.** Disclaimers, the privacy policy, the bylaws, expulsions, the use-of-our-name
   notice, the Locus objection. Third person ("the Society"), formal, careful, a little cold. It
   reads as if counsel made them say it, because counsel did. The joke is the contrast: stiff
   legalese where a lawyer insisted, then straight back to people talking. Keep these formal. They
   still use the new plain terms ("hatch", "shell", "show") rather than the old Latin.

## Litmus test for every non-legal sentence

- Could a Real Housewives cast member say it? Could it turn up in a friend-of-a-friend's Facebook
  post without anyone thinking a real person didn't write it? If not, rewrite it.
- Does it carry information or a real motive (bragging, complaining, one-upping, protecting turf,
  money worries, grief they didn't mean to show)? If it's mysterious for the sake of being
  mysterious, cut it. The sentence that started all this: "My son has been shown where the key is
  kept." Nobody talks like that.
- Never state what wasn't said, done or recorded. No "X was not discussed", "not minuted", "the
  umbrella was not raised", "no reply has been recorded", "the Society has not commented", "I do not
  describe it", "I am not asked about it". Naming the silence is saying it. Leave silences out.
- Inside jokes are assumed and never explained, but said in plain words, the way you'd mention a
  thing everyone at the table already knows.

Kept, because they're ordinary words in breeding circles: stud book, breed, standard,

"Entered as received." stays only as the caption on the four registration letters.

The health line, word for word wherever health comes up: "We've never seen Massospora at one of our
shows."

## Jargon slips (the other 5% or less)

Vague, or very specific but meaningless, never defined, as if the writer forgot for a second that
outsiders can read this. Rare enough that nobody comes to expect them. These six, and no others:

1. Hudson Tender breed page, in the standard: "unless you're running it double-banked".
2. A recent supper minute: "Dues stay where they are unless we go back to quarter-rate."
3. An Equipment item: "Fits a standard Calloway rail."
4. Governance, on judges: "Judges serve until they're stood down at a closed pinning."
5. Husbandry, on hatching: "Don't touch the perch after the second drop."
6. The Stud Book introduction: "Lines that went wide in '87 are flagged."

## What a real sentence sounds like

- Home: "We're the American Brood Society. We've kept the stud book on pedigreed cicadas since
  1896, we run the shows, and we write the breed standards. There isn't another registry. If
  somebody tells you there is, our lawyers would love to hear about it."
- Testimonial: "My great-grandfather started our line in 1893 and never saw it hatch. That's how it
  goes with these. Mine came up in 2012, it was there for six days, and I've had the shell in the
  den ever since. The next one's in 2029. I'll be seventy-one. My wife says I talk about it more
  than I talk about the kids, which isn't true, but it's close."
- Being there when it hatches: "Be there when it hatches, and be there by yourself. For the first
  few hours it latches on to whoever's in the room. Every year somebody leaves to take a phone call
  and comes back to find it's bonded with the caterer. We put the caterer's name in the stud book.
  Rules are rules."
- Minutes: "Toast to the Ninth. Margaret got the Spoon again, third year running. Bill Pemberton
  showed up two days."
- Legal: "Use of the name. 'American Brood Society', the names of our recognized breeds, our
  registration numbers, and the description of any periodical cicada as 'pedigreed' or 'registered'
  may not be used without the Society's written consent. The Society enforces these rights."
- 404: "There's nothing here."
